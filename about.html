Purpose

This project tries to answer a question we faced time and time again at Stanford: what does it look like to make journalism as human as possible in the age of AI?

To us, this means bringing journalism back to its storytelling roots. To the whisper networks of rebellions. To the gossip circles of women's movements. To the town criers ringing their bells.

Problem

City hall meetings are vital public forums, but most local outlets no longer have the resources to send reporters to them. When reporters do cover the smaller city halls, they often produce dry summaries that nobody wants to read — summaries that may keep AI models up to date but do little to help community members find the information they didn't know they needed.

Solution

Good journalism balances three things: accuracy, speed, and engagement. Local papers have over-indexed on the first two. They believed — rationally and rightly — that giving their audiences accurate information swiftly and frequently would be the best way to compete in the digital age.

We have new tools, so we're making a new bet.

We work with AI to help us cover accuracy and speed, so that we can lean into the joy of storytelling. Just because local news is important does not mean it needs to be boring. In fact, if it wants to survive, it can't be!

Deets

Modeled on our favorite road trip podcasts (Crime Junkies and Normal Gossip), Just Do The Right Thing tells you about your city council meetings the way that your friend would.

But journalism needs to be more than just fun — it needs to help you get the information you need to be an effective member of society. Enter AI. The tools we have today allow us to quickly and easily give you the "receipts" you need to check our work or dive deeper into the topic.

Whether you need to know how much they're spending on the park, want to figure out who to vote for, or just gotta win an argument with your neighbor, you have the facts at your fingertips.

The receipts we have today are just an initial offering. If you have ideas for more, or for edits, send them our way!
